# Referee report: "The In-Place Program Is Its Own Specification: Mutable borrows inside a dependent type theory"

## 1. Summary

The paper presents Ochr, a core calculus that puts Rust-style mutable borrows inside a Lean-style dependent type theory. It has inductive types, a definitionally proof-irrelevant impredicative `Prop`, and non-cumulative universes.

Definitional equality is normalisation by a deterministic symbolic machine. That machine is Aeneas's low-level borrow calculus, simplified by treating loans as variables. When a call gets stuck on an abstract value, it is "closed off": its result and the final contents of the places it borrowed become *sealed programs*. These are closed source terms, such as `⌈let c = σ; AddM(&c, 0); c⌉`, that re-run when a later case split refines `σ`. They play the role of Aeneas's backward functions, but are produced lazily, per call, inside conversion.

A derived proposition `Id(A, t, u)` compares two computations by their *observation*: the result plus the final contents of a syntactically computed footprint. It computes to a conjunction of ordinary `Eq`s, and `Eq` itself computes by injectivity and disjointness, in the style of observational type theory (OTT). A recursive call is typed by evaluating the callee's statement in the caller's environment, so induction hypotheses arrive in the shape of the goal. The paper's running example, `AddMZero`, is then proved by bare structural recursion.

The paper's own account is that the central difficulty is keeping two evaluation paths in agreement:

- the definition's generic call, where stuck calls close off;
- each concrete instance, where they run.

It proposes one principle for this: "no decision that changes a result is read from a normal form". It lists the side conditions that principle yields, each with the counterexample that forced it.

On metatheory:

- Consistency, stability (agreement of the two paths) and naturality are stated as conjectures.
- An operational core for a first-order fragment of an *earlier* rule set is mechanised in Lean.
- For a first-order fragment F, soundness is proved on paper, conditional on two assumptions.

The evidence offered is an executable Lean checker (~7k lines), a regression suite with a "counterfactual ledger", a differential fuzzer, and two case studies: Aeneas's hash map, claimed 36% smaller in lines (32% without dead code), and an in-place quicksort.

## 2. Overall assessment

**Weak reject. Confidence: medium.**

The central idea is original and worth publishing. Generating Aeneas's functional translation lazily, as definitional equality, in source syntax, is new. So is obtaining induction hypotheses by evaluating the callee's statement at the call site. The `AddMZero`/`SubM`/`LeAdd` examples in Section 2 are compelling, and the paper is unusually candid about what is and is not established.

However, this is a type theory intended for proofs, and soundness is entirely conjectural for the calculus actually presented and used:

- The one theorem covers a fragment that excludes exactly the features where the paper reports closed proofs of `False`.
- It is conditional on an assumption that, by the paper's own description, "carries most of the operational content" (p. 53).
- The design history shows a recent and repeated pattern: proofs of `False` found by readers, and "fail-safe" classifications later refuted.
- The case studies use constructs outside the formal definition.
- The artifact's checker makes verdict-affecting decisions the paper does not state.
- The headline quantitative claim rests on an asymmetric counting rule.

I would be glad to see this again with at least a proved naturality/stability result for a fragment that includes stuck blocks and generalisation, and with an evaluation that is fair to the baseline.

## 3. Strengths

1. **A genuinely new idea, clearly motivated.** Moving Aeneas's eager, whole-program translation into conversion, lazily and per call, is a fresh point in the design space (p. 2, Section 4.4). Sealed programs as source-level backward functions, and holes for returned borrows filled by the ordinary [End] rule (p. 11), are elegant. Treating loans as variables bound by their borrow is what makes this work.
2. **Induction hypotheses at the call site** (Section 2.4, Section 6.1). Evaluating the callee's statement in the caller's environment, so that the hypothesis arrives wrapped in `S` around the field, is a neat replacement for the frame reasoning a translated-program proof does by hand. `AddZero := AddMZero(&x)` (p. 5) and the `AddSub`/`LeAdd` example (p. 6) show it well: a lemma about the pure function discharges a precondition about mutated state, with no bridging lemma.
3. **`Id` as observational equality of computations** (Section 5). It is a derived proposition with no intro/elim rules, computing to ordinary `Eq`s. The two consequences on p. 13, that owned locals are observed and that unobserved moves are harmless, are thoughtfully argued.
4. **Candour.**
   - Figure 9 is a model of honest status reporting.
   - Section 6.7, Section 7.2 and A.8.8 state plainly what the evidence does not show.
   - The counterexample catalogue (Figure 16, notes 1–11) will be useful to anyone designing effectful dependent type theories.
5. **Engineering.** There is an executable checker, a build-asserted regression suite, a ledger that switches rules off and records which verdicts flip, and a differential fuzzer. I spot-checked the artifact:
   - Every Lean lemma cited in Figure 9 and A.8.3 exists in `ochr/core/meta-lean`.
   - There is exactly one `sorry`: general termination in `GuardTerm.lean`, as stated.
   - Line counts roughly match the paper (meta-lean 11,967 raw lines against "about 11,600"; checker core 7,272 against "about 7,000").
6. **Non-trivial case studies.** The full Aeneas hash-map interface is covered, including `get_mut` and resizing, and quicksort is done with sub-range borrows over an array library.

## 4. Major concerns

### M1. Soundness evidence is too weak for the calculus presented

**What the paper states.**
- Consistency (Conjecture 1), stability (Conjecture 2) and naturality/adequacy (Conjecture 3) are all conjectures (pp. 17–19).
- Theorem 4/19 proves soundness only for fragment F, conditional on two assumptions:
  - Assumption 10, naturality step by step. The paper says it "carries most of the operational content" (p. 53).
  - Assumption 11, transfer of mechanised lemmas from an earlier rule set.
- The earlier rule set's machine differs materially from the current one. It "reads by copying, normalises sealed programs only when states are compared, and has a Unit row in [Close]" (p. 56).
- The `close_*` lemmas carry hypotheses "not yet discharged in Lean" (Figure 9 row 3; p. 57).

**The theorem misses where the bugs were.** F (A.8.1, pp. 53–54) has no stuck blocks, no closures, no user inductives, no pairs, no type families, no `J`/`rewrite`/`split`, and no moves through borrows. These are precisely the features where the paper reports unsoundness:

- generalisation records leaking across arms through a type family (p. 16);
- refinement leaking through a closure's capture (p. 19);
- stuck blocks hiding moves (p. 19);
- class/erasure decisions read from computed types (notes 1–3).

They are also the features every case study uses (`Cells(E, n)`, `WithSplit` with a function argument, dependent fields, `split`, `rewrite`, generic `V`). The theorem therefore gives essentially no assurance about the programs in Section 8.

**The track record is a red flag, and the paper reports it itself.**
- Four closed proofs of `False` were found by readers (pp. 15–16, "the fourth that a cold reader has found").
- Twice, the fuzzer's own analysis called a family fail-safe and was then shown a closed proof of `False` in it (p. 19).
- At one point 15,567 per 10⁶ accepted generated programs failed at runtime (p. 19).
- 65 current findings are classified as fail-safe by the same kind of targeted re-triage (pp. 18–19).
- The last closed `False` "needed two rules the ledger classes as completeness rows" (p. 16). So the ledger's per-rule classification cannot detect interactions. The paper concedes that the ledger shows only necessity (p. 20).

**What would fix it.**
- At minimum, prove Assumption 10 for F. The authors estimate 3–5k lines of Lean (p. 64), which is modest beside the existing 11.6k; why was it not done?
- Re-establish Assumption 11 against the current rules.
- More importantly, extend the result to a fragment that includes stuck blocks and per-arm generalisation, the places where the principle has actually failed.
- Alternatively, mechanise the model for a fragment with type families.

### M2. The formal definition, the checker and the case studies do not coincide

**(a) Constructs used but not formalised.** The abstract says "We give the calculus in full", and p. 29 says "This appendix defines Ochr completely". Yet the case studies rely on constructs that appear nowhere in the formal grammar or rules:
- `abstract` model types, `unsized` slices, `implemented by` natives, and five trusted runtime primitives (p. 22);
- `copy` declarations: referenced on pp. 10 and 31, absent from the declaration grammar of Figure 12 (p. 29) and from [Ind-decl] (p. 46);
- [Open], [Repack] and [Field] (A.4.8, p. 47), which are described only in prose.

The artifact's own rule guard (`RuleGuard.lean`, `expectedIssues`) records that "[Open]" and "[Repack]" are "not printed by the paper".

**(b) Undisclosed checker decisions.** `ochr/core/lean/Ochr/Rules.lean` defines an `Ext` type with 21 constructors. The file describes each as a "decision the checker makes that no printed rule states and that can change a verdict or a normal form". Examples include `CaptureTypeFromValue`, `TypeOfSealed`, `EmbeddedDecl`, `ZeroArmByType`, `BlockEta`, `BlockFieldSplit`, `ConvErrorFalse` and `ConvCycleFalse`.

Several of these read types from values. That sits in direct tension with Section 6.3's central principle and with note 27 (p. 53): "A closure or Π-closure records, with each captured value, the declared type and proof flag of the binding". By contrast, the checker's `capture` records types only for captured proofs. The paper mentions "an extension" exactly once, in the Figure 10 caption.

The same guard lists only seven rules as tagged with `fire` calls; every other rule and every extension is "not yet tagged". This weakens A.9's claim that hovers list "the paper's rules applied there ... by name".

**(c) Undisclosed resource bounds.** The checker has a fuel bound of 2,000,000 steps and depth bounds of 2,000 (`Env.lean:231`, `Machine.lean:231, 2362`), so some verdicts are timeouts. The paper does not say this. That matters, since termination of type checking is open (p. 18).

**What would fix it.** Either state every verdict-affecting decision as a rule in the appendix, or show that each is conservative (can only reject). Report whether the paper's examples and case studies still check with all extensions disabled. Formalise, or explicitly exclude from the soundness claims, the `abstract`/`unsized`/`copy`/native machinery.

### M3. Non-modularity undercuts the thesis for "verified systems code"

Checking is by running, so whether a caller checks depends on the callee's body (p. 3). Conversion unfolds callees, so "extracting a helper function can change which equations hold by `refl`" (pp. 3, 14). There are no opaque definitions with checked bodies (p. 25). The paper contrasts this with Aeneas being modular (p. 23).

For the stated target, verified systems code, modularity is not optional. Once a callee is made opaque, its clients need a specification of it, and the "write it twice" situation the paper sets out to remove returns. Further:

- An opaque borrow-returning definition is an unchecked injectivity assumption (p. 25).
- Ochr refutes a type that safe Rust inhabits, `Π(n : Nat). &Nat` via a leaked `'static` borrow (note 7, p. 51). So Ochr's logic is incompatible with a natural semantics of foreign or library Rust code.

The paper should:
- discuss how the thesis survives modular verification;
- quantify the cost of non-modularity, for example re-checking cost and proof breakage when a callee's implementation changes in the hash map;
- say whether the in-place-is-the-spec benefit persists across an abstraction boundary.

### M4. The headline quantitative comparison is not apples to apples

The abstract leads with "36% smaller in lines than Aeneas's (32% without its dead code)". Several issues compound.

1. **Dead code.** The 36% counts 183 lines of dead code in the baseline against it (p. 20). The headline should be the 32% figure, or lower (see below).
2. **Asymmetric counting rule.**
   - Ochr's rows exclude its 827-line general library (Figure 11 caption).
   - The Aeneas column includes the generic list lemmas in `Hashmap.Properties.fst` (index/map/append/flatten/update lemmas, etc.). The authors' own categorisation script (`notes/hashmap-aeneas-categories.py`) tags these as `P:gen:prop`. By my count of those ranges, roughly 190 non-blank live lines.
   - Arithmetic helpers are also counted on the Aeneas side.
   - For quicksort, the notes show that lemmas were moved from the case study into the `Words` library and thereby left the count (`notes/arrays-library.md`). The caption concedes that Verus's `perm` and swap lemma are counted while Ochr's 126 lines of counting lemmas are not.
3. **Ochr verifies a weaker program.**
   - Numbers are unbounded (unary in the logic, p. 7). Overflow and failure cases account for about 9% of Aeneas's proof lines (p. 22).
   - The load factor is fixed at one rather than configurable.
   - `Get`/`MapGetMut` take a presence proof instead of returning `Option<&mut V>` (p. 22).

   After adjusting for (1)–(3), the margin plausibly falls to around 20%. That is still a result, but a different one.
4. **The baseline is Aeneas's 2022 F*/Z3 development**, not its current Lean backend. The paper positions itself against Lean, so this is the natural comparison. The trusted computing bases are not compared either: Ochr trusts an unverified 7k-line checker whose soundness is conjectural, plus the extensions above. Aeneas-Lean trusts its translation plus a small kernel.
5. **Inconsistent framing.** Quicksort is 8× Verus (867 vs 108 lines). The paper says this "is not a test of the thesis ... it measures what SMT automation buys" (p. 23). But the hash-map baseline is also SMT-discharged, and there the comparison is presented as support for the thesis. Moreover, Verus states its specs about the executable code directly, with no model and no agreement layer, so it is arguably the closer test of "one artifact instead of three".
6. **No effort measure.** Line counts alone are reported. The paper says so (p. 21), but then the thesis claim on p. 2, "of comparable size to the three pieces", is all the evaluation supports.

**What would fix it.** Use a symmetric counting rule, either counting or excluding general libraries on both sides. Give a sensitivity table showing which adjustments move the margin. Compare against Aeneas-Lean. Add a short TCB discussion.

### M5. Positioning overstates the problem for auto-active Rust verifiers

Section 1.1's "two-language problem" describes Aeneas accurately. It describes Verus, Creusot and Prusti less well: there the user specifies and proves properties of the executable code itself, and verification conditions are generated, not hand-written refinement proofs. Ochr's real distinction from those tools is foundational: conversion-based, no solver, proofs as terms in a dependent type theory. It is not "one artifact versus three". The introduction and Section 9 should say this crisply.

Related work should also engage with:
- Idris 2 / QTT in practice (linear in-place update with dependent types);
- Mezzo;
- Pédrot and Tabareau's exceptional type theories, as further points on effects in dependent type theory beyond the fire triangle.

It should also compare the expressiveness of per-call holes with prophecies (per region, borrows in data) more concretely than the paragraph on p. 23 does.

### M6. The body is not self-contained, and the paper reads as a design log

The essential definitions all live only in a 37-page appendix:
- footprints and owners (A.1.4);
- the precise [Access] sets;
- [Drop];
- the generic call (A.1.4 item 10);
- [Split-gen];
- stuck-block captures and modes (A.2.8);
- the erasure classification (A.4.1).

The body's only rule figure (Figure 8) has four rules. A referee cannot check any non-trivial claim from the 25-page body.

Large parts of Section 6 and the appendix narrate design history:
- "Two independent reviews of an earlier version" (p. 15);
- "the fourth that a cold reader has found" (p. 16);
- "An earlier version had a third row" (note 15);
- internal decision identifiers D53, D58 and D68 (pp. 45, 54, 55, 58, 60, 63);
- "the then 20 soundness rows" (p. 19).

The candour is valuable. But the presentation should separate the definition from its genealogy and give the reader one stable system. A worked derivation of `AddMZero` in the appendix rules would help a great deal.

## 5. Minor concerns

- **p. 25 (Section 10.2) contradicts p. 10 and A.8.**
  - Section 10.2 says: "Erased reads do not count as uses, which is what lets `x + x = 2 · x` be stated without `clone`."
  - p. 10 says: "`Add(x, x)` must be written `Add(clone(x), x)`, in a type as in code."
  - p. 63 says: "Since D68 an observation reads as runtime code does ... `Id(Nat, let z = a; a, a)` is rejected."
  - p. 24's "a variable used in a type is not consumed" also needs reconciling.
- **Abstract.** "Its operational core is mechanised for a first-order fragment" omits "of an earlier rule set" (contrast p. 3 and Section 7.3). The mechanised machine differs (p. 56).
- **p. 11.** "This correspondence is proved for the first-order fragment ... (property 3)". Figure 9 says "mechanised, with hypotheses" that are not discharged, for the earlier rule set. Use "mechanised under hypotheses".
- **p. 39, η for `Unit`.** Values are replaced by `()` when a call's "computed result type" is `Unit`. This decision is read from a computed type, which seems to sit uneasily with the Section 6.3 principle, and the fourth closed `False` (p. 16) passed through this rule. Please explain why the current form is safe.
- **p. 10, closure semantics.** "A closure that assigns a captured variable assigns its own copy, afresh at each call", so calling twice gives the same result twice. This matches no Rust closure kind. Reconcile it with Section 10.2's claim that the machine "moves data as Rust does".
- **p. 22, quicksort recursion on fuel.** State explicitly that fuel `n` suffices, so that the theorem is about real quicksort. Say how measure-based recursion would interact with [Rec]'s syntactic strict-subterm check.
- **Figure 1 / p. 11, hole imprecision.** "The hole goes into every borrowed argument": a call returning a borrow into its first argument keeps its second borrowed too. How often does this reject idiomatic code? Is there an example from the case studies?
- **p. 47 (A.5).** It says "the four conditions of Section 7.1, spelled out, plus typing", but six are listed. Also, "That the machine preserves conditions 1 to 5 is property 1": condition 4 (captures) cannot be covered by a mechanisation without closures.
- **p. 19.** "17 of the then 20 soundness rows" versus Figure 10's 23. Re-run the fuzzer against the current ledger, or report both.
- **p. 39, conversion.**
  - Is ≡ transitive? (Lean's defeq is not; is Ochr's?)
  - Is [Conv-fun] decidable?
  - The checker answers "not convertible" on error or cycle (`ConvErrorFalse`, `ConvCycleFalse`). The paper should say so, since it determines completeness.
- **p. 7, `Word`.** `Word` is "unary in the logic, a machine word in the cost model". The verified properties therefore do not speak about u64 semantics. Unary `Idx(k, n)` arithmetic could also affect checking time on realistic inputs.
- **p. 60.** `exec_total` is cited in the proof of Lemma 17 but is absent from Figure 9 and the A.8.3 table.
- **p. 8, Figure 3.** It omits `clone(p)` and `rewrite ← h`, which Figure 12 has and Section 2 uses.

## 6. Presentation and readability

The informal overview (Section 2) is the best part of the paper. Each idea is introduced through a small program, and the AddM → AddMZero → TailM → SubM progression builds intuition well.

From Section 4 onward the prose becomes very dense:
- definitions are given as long sentences with many clauses;
- more than fifteen bespoke concepts accumulate: closing off, sealed program, stuck block, generic call, footprint, owners, fill, hole, inert, whole, resolution, refinement, generalisation record, declared type/declared sort, class, proof position, nestable parameter, and others;
- forward references are frequent.

Section 6.3–6.7 and Figure 16 read as a list of patches, each justified by a counterexample. That is informative, but it makes the system feel unstable.

Specific problems:
- The `⇝` arrow is overloaded: it denotes [End] steps (p. 33) and the tail case-tree judgement (p. 44).
- Theorem 4 (body) and Theorem 19 (appendix) are the same result under different numbers; say so.
- The paper is 65 pages, and the essential content is in the appendix (see M6).
- Code listings overflow (see Section 8).
- Several appendix rules overrun the margins (see Section 8).

I recommend:
1. a compact formal core in the body: syntax, the machine judgement, [Close], [Obs]/[Id], [Call-type], [Split] and [Def], with the auxiliary definitions;
2. one worked derivation;
3. moving the design history into a clearly separated "lessons/counterexamples" section.

## 7. Questions for the authors

1. Assumption 10 for F is estimated at 3–5k lines of Lean (p. 64). What prevented proving it? Can stability and naturality be proved for F plus stuck blocks plus per-arm generalisation, the places where the principle failed?
2. Why is there no declarative system (p. 14)? Without one, what specification is the checker complete with respect to? How would you state "the checker implements Ochr" other than "the checker is Ochr"?
3. Of the 21 `Ext` decisions in `Rules.lean`, which can change a verdict on the paper's examples or case studies? Do the hash map and quicksort still check with all of them disabled?
4. How does the "program is its own specification" thesis survive the addition of opaque definitions? What does a client of an opaque `InsertM` prove against?
5. Given Ochr refutes `Π(n : Nat). &Nat` being inhabited (note 7), how would Ochr interface with `unsafe`/foreign code or standard-library functions such as `Box::leak`?
6. Is conversion transitive? Is it decidable on the fragment the case studies use? How often do the case studies approach the fuel or depth bounds?
7. How large do sealed programs grow? For deep recursions, nested sealed programs are re-normalised at each refinement. What is the checking-time behaviour as input sizes in statements grow?
8. Can you report the hash-map comparison under a symmetric counting rule, and against Aeneas's Lean backend (which has an analogous hash-map development)?
9. In Section 10.2, do erased reads count as uses or not (see the minor concern above)?
10. What are the semantics of `abstract`, `unsized`, `implemented by` and `copy`? Are they within the scope of Conjectures 1–3?
11. Why is η for `Unit` keyed on computed result types safe under the Section 6.3 principle?
