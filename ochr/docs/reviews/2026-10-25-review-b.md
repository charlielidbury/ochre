# Referee report: "The In-Place Program Is Its Own Specification: Mutable borrows inside a dependent type theory" (65 pp.)

## 1. Summary

The paper presents Ochr, a core calculus that puts Rust-style mutable borrows inside a Lean-style dependent type theory. Ochr has inductive types, an impredicative proof-irrelevant Prop, and a primitive structural Eq. Definitional equality is normalisation by a deterministic call-by-value symbolic machine, which descends from Aeneas's low-level borrow calculus. When a call gets stuck on an abstract value, it is "closed off". Its result, and the final contents of the places it borrowed, become "sealed programs": closed source programs that re-run when a later case split refines the abstract value. These play the role of Aeneas's backward functions, but they are produced lazily and in the source language. A derived proposition Id(A, t, u) compares two computations by what they return and what they write, read off a syntactic footprint, and computes to a conjunction of ordinary Eq. Because a callee's statement is evaluated at the call site, induction hypotheses arrive with the frame already applied. The paper argues that the main design problem is keeping two evaluation paths in agreement: one at a definition's generic call, the other at each instance. It proposes one principle to do this: no decision that changes a result is read from a normal form. It catalogues the side conditions that follow, each with the counterexample that forced it. On the evidence side:

- Consistency, stability and naturality are conjectures.
- Soundness is proved on paper only for a small first-order fragment, and only conditionally on a step-wise naturality assumption.
- Parts of the operational core are mechanised in Lean, for an earlier rule set.
- There is an executable Lean checker with a regression suite, a "counterfactual ledger" and a differential fuzzer.
- There are two case studies. Aeneas's hash map comes out 36% smaller in lines than Aeneas's F* development. An in-place quicksort comes out 8 times larger than Verus's.

## 2. Overall assessment

**Weak reject. Confidence: medium-high.** I read every page and checked several claims against the artefact.

The ideas are original and interesting. Closing off into sealed source programs, Id over computations that reduces to ordinary equations, and call-site evaluation of induction hypotheses as automatic frame reasoning all look like real contributions. The paper is also unusually candid about what is not proved.

For a top venue, though, the evidence does not yet support the central claims:

- The calculus's consistency is conjectural.
- Cold readers have broken it with closed proofs of False four times, and the fuzzer twice more.
- The one soundness theorem is conditional on an assumption that "carries most of the operational content". It covers a fragment that excludes everything the case studies use.
- The mechanisation targets an earlier rule set.
- I found several places where the paper and the artefact disagree. This matters because the paper's methodology is "the checker is the definition".
- The empirical comparison rests on one case study against a 2022 baseline, with asymmetries that are only partly disclosed.

I would like to see this work again, either with a soundness result for a fragment that covers the case studies, or reframed explicitly as a design study.

## 3. Strengths

- **Closing off (Section 4.4, Figure 6).** This is an elegant mechanism. Backward functions are recovered on demand, in source syntax, and "re-run when refined" (Section 4.5). It is a simple, uniform answer to how a stuck effectful call can be a neutral. The treatment of returned borrows as a hole substituted by the ordinary [End] rule (p. 11) is neat, and it exploits the "loans are variables" departure from Aeneas well (p. 9).
- **Id as equality of observations (Section 5, Figure 7).** Id computes to ordinary Eq conjunctions, so it needs no introduction or elimination rules of its own. That is a clean design. The "owned locals are observed" and "unobserved moves are harmless" discussions (p. 13) show the semantic choices were thought through.
- **The running example (Sections 2.2–2.4).** It is very well chosen. Seeing AddMZero's induction hypothesis arrive "already wrapped in S" (p. 5) makes the frame-argument-by-evaluation idea concrete.
- **The organising principle (Section 6.3).** "No decision that changes a result is read from a normal form" is crisp and reusable. It should transfer to other dependent type theories whose conversion runs effectful code.
- **The counterexample catalogue (Figure 16 and notes 1–27, pp. 49–53).** It is valuable in itself. Each side condition comes with the closed proof of False it prevents. Few type-theory papers document why their side conditions exist this concretely.
- **Honesty about status.** Figure 9 (p. 17) separates mechanised from conjectured claims carefully. Theorem 19 ends with "This theorem reduces soundness of F to Assumption 10 and Assumption 11; it is not a proof of soundness outright" (p. 62).
- **Hash map study.** It is carried out in detail. I confirmed its observation about Aeneas's remove lemma: `hash_map_remove_back_lem` ensures `hash_map_t_inv self` rather than `hm'` (competitors/aeneas-hashmap/icfp22/fstar/Hashmap.Properties.fsti:255).
- **Artefact engineering.** Build-time verdict assertions with per-file counts (p. 20), the ledger's switch-per-rule design, and editor integration (A.9) make the checker a good tool for exploring the design.

## 4. Major concerns

### M1. The soundness evidence does not match the claims

**Where:** abstract; p. 2 ("the evidence that it is sound"); Section 7 (pp. 16–19); A.8 (pp. 53–65).

**Problem.**
- Consistency (Conjecture 1), stability (Conjecture 2) and naturality/adequacy (Conjecture 3) are all conjectures.
- The mechanisation covers "an earlier rule set, which predates the erasure and inductive-proposition rules of Section 6" (p. 19). Its close_* lemmas carry hypotheses "not yet discharged in Lean" (Figure 9, row 3).
- Theorem 4/19 is proved on paper for a fragment F (p. 53). F has only Nat, Unit, True, False, And, Eq and Id. It has no user inductives, pairs, closures, stuck blocks, J, rewrite, split, or moves through borrows.
- The theorem is also conditional on Assumption 10, which "carries most of the operational content" (p. 53) and whose "risks we know of" are listed on p. 56, and on Assumption 11, which transfers lemmas from the earlier rule set.
- Every case study lies outside F. Both use user inductives (Tree, List, Opt, HashMap, Cells). They also use generics (V : Type), a closure argument to WithSplit, split, rewrite, dependent fields and abstract types.

**Why it matters.**
- The paper's own history argues that the rules are not yet stable. Section 6.4 (p. 16) reports "the fourth [closed proof of False] that a cold reader has found". The fuzzer found False twice more (p. 19).
- Section 7.2 (p. 18) concedes that "each earlier list of decisions also missed one, most recently item (5)".
- Section 6.7 (p. 16) concedes that the ledger shows necessity, not sufficiency.
- With conversion running effectful code on two paths, the hard cases are exactly the ones excluded from F.
- Calling the hash map "verified" (abstract) is therefore conditional on an open conjecture, and the paper should say so where the claim is made.

**What would fix it.** Any of the following would change my assessment:
- a proof, paper or mechanised, of Conjecture 2 or of consistency via the sketched set model (A.6), for a fragment that includes user inductives, returned borrows and [Split]/stuck blocks;
- discharging Assumption 10 for F (the authors estimate 3–5k lines of Lean, p. 64) and porting the mechanisation to the current rules;
- failing those, reframing the paper as a design paper whose case studies are evidence of expressiveness, not of verification.

### M2. The formal definition and the artefact diverge, which undermines "the checker is the definition"

**Where:** pp. 14, 16, 18–20, 25, 29, 49–51.

**Problem.** The paper says there is "no separate declarative system of which the checker is an algorithm" (p. 14). Appendix A "defines Ochr completely" (p. 29). Fidelity between paper and checker is therefore the soundness story, and I found these discrepancies:

1. **Missing rules.** The case studies depend on rules absent from Appendix A. Section 8.3 (p. 22) relies on `abstract` types, `unsized` types and `implemented by` natives. The artefact's RULES.md has rules [Abstract], [Implemented-by] and [Unsized] (D57) for them. Appendix A has none of these. The hash map is built on this arrays library, so both case studies use undocumented rules.
2. **Stale confinement claims.** Section 8.1 (p. 20) says "Confinement backs up the reading of proofs by their declaration: with both off, a false lemma is accepted". Note 9 (p. 51) is titled "Confinement is a fail-safe", and the Figure 10 caption mentions a "confining" extension. Commit 99ef2f39 (2026-10-01) deleted confinement (D41) from the checker, together with its switches and ledger rows.
3. **Ledger counts.** Figure 10 (p. 20) lists 23 soundness, 1 false-lemma, 4 model, 9 policy and 24 completeness rows (61 in all). The artefact's asserted ledger (Ochr/Examples/Registry.lean, `rowClass`) has 23 soundness, 6 model, 8 policy and 21 completeness rows (58), with no false-lemma class.
4. **Necessity of Figure 16's first condition.** Section 6.7 says the ledger shows each Figure 16 condition necessary. Yet "erasure by declared type" (Figure 16's first row, note 1) has no ledger row: Ledger.lean records that switching it off flips nothing. BoomL is a regression test whose original switch "is gone" (Examples/Erasure.lean).
5. **Contradiction on erased reads.** Section 10.2 (p. 25) says "Erased reads do not count as uses, which is what lets x + x = 2 · x be stated without clone". This contradicts p. 10 ("Add(x, x) must be written Add(clone(x), x), in a type as in code") and p. 63 ("Id(Nat, let z = a; a, a) is rejected").
6. **Fuzzer numbers from a different rule version.** The quoted run (p. 18: 10⁶ cases, 1,165 s, 65 findings in one class) is identified in notes/fuzzer.md as checker c469da44 "with D53 off by default". That means runtime reads did not move, unlike the rules presented in Section 4.1. The later D68 change ("reads in statements move", each Eq side on its own copy) postdates it. The phrase "17 of the then 20 soundness rows" (p. 19) is likewise stale against 23 rows. A run with the move semantics on does exist in the notes (0 findings in 10⁶), so this is more about accuracy than a hidden failure. But the paper should quote numbers for the rules it presents.

**Why it matters.** When the definition is the checker and the evidence is the ledger plus the fuzzer, stale text and missing rules directly weaken the soundness argument. Readers cannot tell which rule set the evidence covers.

**What would fix it.**
- Add the missing rules to Appendix A.
- Regenerate every quoted number (ledger, fuzzer, line counts) from one tagged commit.
- Ideally, have the build check the paper's numbers.
- Remove the confinement text.

### M3. The empirical comparison does not yet support "comparable size, no model, no agreement proof"

**Where:** abstract; p. 2; Section 8.2 (pp. 20–22); Figure 11; Section 8.3.

**Problems.**

(a) **Old baseline.** The baseline is Aeneas's 2022 F* development (p. 20). Aeneas's current Lean backend, with its proof automation, is the state of the art and is not compared.

(b) **The programs and obligations differ.**
- Ochr's numbers are unbounded, so overflow and failure handling disappear. By the paper's own count that is about 3% + 6% of Aeneas's lines (p. 22).
- The load factor is fixed at one rather than Aeneas's 4/5.
- Words are "unary in the logic, a machine word in the cost model" (p. 7). Properties proved about unbounded words do not hold of a program running on machine words. This is a semantic gap, not only a counting asymmetry.

(c) **Library counting.** Ochr's 827-line general library is excluded; Aeneas's Primitives.fst is 222 lines. notes/hashmap-case-study.md reports that with both libraries counted the totals are 2,523 against 2,892. That is about 13% smaller, not 36%. The paper should report this number.

(d) **Lines are not effort.** The paper admits it writes out every case split that Z3 discharges for Aeneas, and has no effort measure (pp. 21–22).

(e) **Language tuned to the case study.** The first complete version's proofs were 1.05 times Aeneas's. They shrank by almost a quarter through rewrite, destructuring let and split, all added during the study (pp. 20–21). That shrinkage is partly the language being fitted to this case study.

(f) **Only one data point supports the thesis.** The quicksort study is 8 times Verus (p. 23).

**What would fix it.**
- Compare against the Aeneas Lean hash map.
- Report library-inclusive totals.
- Treat machine words faithfully or drop the cost-model claim.
- Add a second case study chosen before the language was tuned to it.
- Measure effort, even roughly.

### M4. Framing: "the program is its own specification", and modularity

**Where:** title; Sections 1.1, 1.5, 6.2, 8.2, 10.1.

**Specifications still exist.**
- Ochr theorems are still stated with specification functions: Find, Count, Sorted, Size, OnlyIn, Counted, Entries.
- Figure 11 itself counts 184 and 30 lines of "Spec, model" for Ochr.
- What is really eliminated is the separate refinement layer: the translated model and the agreement proof. The title and abstract should say that.

**When the specification is the code.**
- Algebraic laws among a structure's own operations can be satisfied by consistently wrong implementations. For example, Find is the in-place bucket walk run on a copy (p. 21).
- Changing the implementation changes what the theorems mean.
- The paper should discuss what assurance a user gets.

**Proofs depend on bodies.**
- Section 1.5 and Section 6.2 state that conversion has no δ-rule for stuck calls.
- Callers' typing depends on callee bodies.
- "extracting a helper function can change which equations hold by refl".
- Opaque definitions are not supported (Section 10.1).

**Why it matters.** The motivation is verified systems code. Non-modular checking, together with proofs that are fragile under refactoring, is a central scalability concern, and the paper never evaluates it. For example, how many hash-map proofs break under a behaviour-preserving refactoring?

**Rust semantics.** Section 10.1 and note 7 point out that Ochr proves every Π(x : &Nat).&Nat has an injective backward function, which is false for safe Rust (Box::leak). Opaque or foreign functions would therefore be unchecked axioms. This deserves more prominence, since modular verification needs exactly such functions.

### M5. Presentation obstructs checking the claims

**Where:** whole paper.

**Problem.**
- The body has only three rule figures (Figures 5, 6, 8). The notions needed to follow Sections 5–6 are in the appendix: owners, footprint order, stuck-block capture modes, generalisation records, [Tail-*].
- The appendix (37 pp.) is the actual definition. The prose is dense and idiosyncratic ("fills", "holes", "two paths", "cold reader"), with many forward references.
- Development history is mixed into technical sections: "Two independent reviews of an earlier version" (p. 15); "the fourth that a cold reader has found" (p. 16); "then 20 soundness rows" (p. 19); "Since D68" (pp. 60, 63).
- Internal decision ids leak into the text: (D58) p. 45; (D68)/(D53) pp. 54, 55, 58.
- Conjecture 2 (p. 18) is informal. "At every subterm both runs reach" needs a definition of how subterms align between a run that closes a call off and one that unfolds it.

**What would fix it.**
- Move a compact rule summary of the machine, observation and [Split]/[Close] into the body.
- Formalise Conjecture 2's statement.
- Move process history to an artefact appendix.

## 5. Minor concerns

- **p. 10, Section 4.3.** Closures that assign a captured variable get "its own copy, afresh at each call". Rust's FnMut accumulates; Fn rejects. Ochr thus accepts programs Rust rejects, with a different semantics. Please say how compiled code would implement this, given Section 10.2's assumption that compiled code behaves like the checked machine.
- **p. 11.** "This correspondence is proved for the first-order fragment … (property 3)". Figure 9 marks property 3 as "mechanised, with hypotheses … not yet in Lean". Say "proved under hypotheses".
- **p. 13 / A.1.4 item 5 (p. 32).** ∧ is not commutative by conversion, and the footprint order is "first for t and then u". Is Id(A, t, u) ≡ Id(A, u, t)? If not, state that symmetry needs a lemma.
- **p. 13.** "Owned locals are observed" means a dead store to a local is not Id-equal to (). That is fine, but say explicitly that Id is not contextual equivalence modulo liveness.
- **p. 17 / A.6.** Is function extensionality provable or refutable? [Conv-fun] compares generic observations, so the answer is not obvious.
- **p. 18.** "the checker rejects no true statement the fuzzer poses". The fuzzer notes report about 47k rejected cases. Explain how truth is established and which rejections are excluded.
- **p. 19.** Conjecture 3 is stated "up to resolution", which costs completeness (the Pick example). It would help to state the completeness cost in general.
- **p. 21, Figure 11 caption.** Give the library-inclusive totals next to the library-exclusive ones.
- **p. 22.** The five array natives are trusted, and their models are not checked against implementations. State the size of this trusted interface.
- **p. 23.** Quicksort recurses on fuel. Say that termination is therefore not established for the in-place program.
- **p. 1 vs p. 20.** The intro says Aeneas "translates safe Rust into a pure Lean program", but the comparison uses the F* backend.
- **Theorem numbering.** Theorem 4 (p. 19) and Theorem 19 (p. 60) are the same result with different numbers. Use one number.
- **Trusted base.** Please state explicitly what a user trusts: the roughly 7,000-line unverified checker, the natives, and Conjectures 1–3. Contrast that with Aeneas's trusted translation plus F*/Z3.

## 6. Presentation and readability

**What works.** Section 2 is the strongest part: the examples are well chosen and build intuition step by step. Figures 1 and 9 are excellent for setting scope and status.

**Sections 3–7.** These are hard going. Concepts are introduced in prose with forward pointers into the appendix, and the main text often states a rule's effect without the rule.

**The appendix.** It is thorough, and the `checker:` cross-references to the Lean source are a nice touch. However:
- several rules overflow the margins (see Section 8 of this report);
- the notes in A.7 mix "what goes wrong without it" with history.

**Length.** At 25 + 40 pages, length is acceptable for a journal-style artefact, but the body is not self-contained.

**Bibliography.** It has a duplicate entry and LaTeX escapes that were not rendered (details below).

## 7. Questions for the authors

1. Which commit do the ledger, fuzzer and line-count numbers in the paper come from? Has the 10⁶-case fuzzer run been repeated on the final rules, with reads that move, D68, and arm-scoped generalisation records?
2. Which part of Assumption 10 do you expect to fail first when F is widened to user inductives and stuck blocks? Is Conjecture 2 provable for F as it stands?
3. How does the hash-map comparison change against Aeneas's current Lean development?
4. How are unbounded Words in the logic reconciled with machine words at runtime? Does a verified Ochr program correspond to a Rust program using usize?
5. How robust are proofs to semantics-preserving refactoring of implementations, given there is no δ-rule and no opaque definitions? Did you measure this on the hash map?
6. Can a user state an implementation-independent interface specification for an abstract or opaque function and use it modularly? If so, how is the injectivity principle of note 7 discharged for it?
7. Is function extensionality, or its negation, derivable? Is Id symmetric definitionally?
8. In Conjecture 2, how are the subterms of the closed-off run matched with those of the direct run?
9. Have you observed nontermination of type checking in practice? Does the checker use fuel?
10. Is a declarative presentation possible? Without one, what form would subject reduction or a substitution lemma take?
