# Referee report: "The In-Place Program Is Its Own Specification: Mutable borrows inside a dependent type theory"

## 1. Summary

The paper presents Ochr, a core calculus that puts Rust-style mutable borrows into a Lean-style dependent type theory. It has inductive types, an impredicative proof-irrelevant `Prop` whose connectives are inductive declarations, and a primitive `Eq` that computes in the style of observational type theory.

Definitional equality is evaluation by a deterministic symbolic machine taken from Aeneas's low-level borrow calculus. The machine has one change: a loan is a variable bound by its borrow. When a call gets stuck on an abstract value, it is "closed off". Its result, and the final contents of each place it borrowed, become sealed programs: closed source programs that re-run when a later case split refines the abstract value. These play the role of Aeneas's backward functions, computed lazily in source syntax. When the call returns a borrow, a "hole" stands for that borrow's final value.

A derived proposition `Id(A, t, u)` compares two computations by their result and by the final contents of a syntactic footprint of owned places. It computes to a conjunction of ordinary `Eq`s. Theorems about in-place code, such as `AddM(x, 0)` having no effect, are then proved by structural recursion. The induction hypothesis arrives already framed, because the callee's statement is evaluated in the caller's environment.

The authors identify the central difficulty: every statement is evaluated along two paths, at the generic call and directly at each instance. They organise the side conditions under one principle: "no decision that changes a result is read from a normal form". Each side condition is paired with the counterexample (a closed proof of `False`) that forced it.

The evidence is:

- a Lean reference checker (about 7k lines) whose rules are tagged by the paper's rule names;
- a "counterfactual ledger" that switches each rule off;
- a differential fuzzer;
- a Lean mechanisation (about 11.6k lines) of a first-order fragment of an *earlier* rule set;
- a paper proof of soundness for a small fragment F. This proof is conditional on a naturality assumption and on transferring the mechanised lemmas to the current rules.

Consistency, stability and naturality are stated as conjectures. There are two case studies:

- Aeneas's resizing hash map, in a development 36% smaller in lines than Aeneas's 2022 F* development;
- an in-place quicksort over a cons-cell array model, about 8x the size of Verus's.

## 2. Overall assessment

**Recommendation: weak reject.**

**Confidence: high.**

The core idea is original and well worth publishing at some point. Computing Aeneas-style backward functions lazily, inside conversion, as source-level sealed programs, and then stating theorems about in-place code directly through an observational `Id`, is clever. The overview examples are compelling. The paper is also unusually honest about its limitations.

But for a venue like POPL, the central technical claim, that this is a sound dependent type theory, is unsupported beyond a conjecture:

- The design has a documented history of reactive patching. Four closed proofs of `False` were found by "cold readers" and two more by the fuzzer. The most recent came from an interaction of two rules that were each individually classed as harmless.
- The only theorem is conditional on an assumption that the paper itself says "carries most of the operational content". It also covers a fragment that excludes nearly every feature that makes Ochr interesting.
- The mechanisation is of a different, earlier machine that reads by copying rather than moving.
- The empirical comparison with Aeneas uses an outdated baseline and a program with different semantics (for example, unbounded integers).

I would be enthusiastic about a version that either proves the naturality/stability step for a meaningful fragment, or is reframed and scoped as a design paper with correspondingly modest claims.

## 3. Strengths

- **Genuinely new idea.** Making Aeneas's functional translation a lazy part of definitional equality, in the source language, is novel (pp. 2, 11). "Closing off" a stuck effectful call into sealed programs, plus holes for returned borrows filled by the ordinary [End] rule, is an elegant mechanism. Treating loans as variables bound by their borrows makes [End] a substitution and makes holes almost free (p. 9, p. 11).
- **Excellent motivating examples.** `AddMZero` (pp. 1, 5) shows the induction hypothesis arriving "already wrapped in S" because the callee's statement is evaluated in the caller's environment. This makes the frame argument definitional. The `SubM`/`AddSub` example (p. 6), where a proof about the pure `Add` is accepted where a proof about the mutated state is required, is a convincing demonstration of the payoff.
- **Clear statement of the central difficulty.** The paper frames it as two evaluation paths that must agree (§1.3, §6.3), and gives a crisp organising principle. It is an honest and useful framing for anyone designing effectful conversion.
- **Unusual candour.** The paper says plainly what is proved, what is conjectured, and where it failed (Fig. 9, §6.4, §6.7, §7.2, A.8.8). Statements such as "An argument about a class of findings is not a proof" (p. 19) and "The ledger shows that each rule is needed for some verdict, not that the rules suffice" (p. 20) are exactly right.
- **Valuable catalogue of counterexamples.** The counterexamples in A.7 (notes 1–11) are a real contribution in themselves. Anyone combining effects, erasure and dependent types will benefit from `LieL`, `LieB`, `LieH`, `Bad5`, `PickX/PickY` and `PF/QF`. The coinductive-[Conv-fun] counterexample (p. 39) is also instructive.
- **Faithful artefact engineering.** Every rule is tagged with the checker function that implements it. The build asserts verdict counts, so a silently empty file fails, and the ledger is enforced. I spot-checked several claims against the artefact and they hold:
  - the Aeneas `hash_map_remove_back_lem` really does assert `hash_map_t_inv self` rather than of `hm'` (p. 21);
  - the line counts in Fig. 11 reproduce the 36%/32%/29% figures;
  - the named Lean theorems exist, and the only `sorry` is in general termination.
- **Careful model-level reasoning.** The Ochr-specific constraints are explained well:
  - why borrows are restricted to `Type₀` (note 11, the System U⁻ argument);
  - why borrow-returning function types need a borrow parameter (note 7, the leaked `'static` borrow);
  - why subsingleton elimination is needed for the model even though no `False` proof forces it (note 10).

## 4. Major concerns

### M1. Soundness of the calculus is conjectural, and the evidence pattern argues against treating the current rules as settled

*Where:* p. 2 §1.3; pp. 15–16 §6.4; p. 16 §6.7; Fig. 9 (p. 17); pp. 18–19 §7.2; A.8 (pp. 53–65).

**The design method is counterexample-driven.** Every side condition "was forced by a counterexample" (p. 2). Six closed proofs of `False` or unsound acceptances are reported:

- two from the first independent reviews (p. 15);
- a third cold-reader `False`, mentioned on p. 16 but never described;
- a fourth, through a generalisation record shared across arms together with η for `Unit` (p. 16);
- two from the fuzzer: through a closure's capture, and through generalisation records again (p. 19).

The fourth arose from "two rules that on their own only admit more good programs" (p. 16). Twice, "the fuzzer's own analysis had called the family fail-safe" (p. 19). Each earlier list of stability decisions "also missed one, most recently item (5)" (p. 18). Fuzzing also found that accepted programs failed at runtime 15,567 times in 10⁶ cases before fixes (p. 19).

This is a design whose failures were found by looking, and there is no argument that the search is finished. The current evidence for sufficiency is:

- the fuzzer, which by the paper's own account has twice mis-triaged;
- the ledger, which by construction shows only necessity;
- Theorem 4.

**Theorem 4 / Theorem 19 is very weak evidence for the calculus as presented.**

- It is conditional on Assumption 10, "naturality, step by step". By the paper's own description this "carries most of the operational content" (p. 53). The listed risks (p. 56) are exactly the delicate parts: early ends, preservation of (A3)/(A5) by [Close], and (N3) through [Seal] with inert loans.
- It is also conditional on Assumption 11, transfer from a different machine (see M2).
- The fragment F (pp. 53–54) excludes user inductives and pairs, closures and Π-values, stuck blocks, `J`, `rewrite`, `split`, type families, `Prop` parameters, matches inside types, and reads through borrows. F6 rules out the `*x := p` in `SubM` and the `mem::replace` pattern.
- So F covers essentially `AddM`/`AddMZero`/`TailM`. None of the constructs implicated in the six reported soundness failures, and neither case study, is in F.

**Why it matters.** The paper's title-level claim is a dependent type theory usable as a logic (proofs of `Sorted ∧ Perm`, hash map invariants). Without consistency, or at least a stability/naturality result covering the features the case studies use, a reader cannot tell whether the case studies are meaningful.

**What would fix it.** Either of:

- discharge Assumption 10 for F (the authors estimate 3–5k lines of Lean, p. 64), and widen F to include at least stuck blocks and user inductives, since those are where the failures were;
- reframe the paper explicitly as a design paper whose contribution is the mechanism, the principle and the counterexample catalogue, and drop or soften "the evidence that it is sound" (p. 2).

### M2. The mechanisation is of a different machine than the one presented

*Where:* Abstract; p. 3 (Contributions); p. 19 §7.3; p. 56 (Assumption 11 and the paragraph after it).

The mechanised machine:

- "predates the erasure and inductive-proposition rules";
- "reads by copying";
- "normalises sealed programs only when states are compared";
- "has a Unit row in [Close]".

Reading by copying versus moving is not a cosmetic difference. Moves are central to Rust and to this machine: [Read]/[Move], ⊥, wholeness at [End], stuck blocks hiding moves. They are where the fuzzer found the 15,567/10⁶ runtime failures (p. 19). Properties 1–4 of Fig. 9 are therefore about a sibling calculus.

The abstract says only "Its operational core is mechanised for a first-order fragment". The qualifier "of an earlier rule set" appears in the contributions list but not in the abstract.

**Fix.** Port `exec_wf`, `frame_local`/`call_effect` and the `close_*` lemmas to moving reads and the current [Close] (A.8.8 item 2), or state the gap prominently in the abstract and in Fig. 9.

### M3. Conjecture 2 does not appear to be the statement the soundness argument needs

*Where:* p. 18 Conjecture 2; p. 19 the `Pick` example and Conjecture 3; A.8.2 Definition 9 (p. 55); Lemma 14 (p. 58); "Remarks on stability" (p. 63).

Conjecture 2 compares the run of `t` at Ω, then α, against the run of `tα` at Ωα. Item (6) asserts that the footprint at Ω *contains* the one at Ωα.

But the appendix's own analysis shows that a direct (ground) run does not reach Ωα. It reaches a state that agrees with Ωα only up to resolution, because the symbolic path may end borrows earlier (the `Pick` example, A.8.7). Footprints then "may differ in both directions" (p. 63, item 6; Lemma 14, where the ground side's extras come from borrow variables the symbolic path already ended). So either Conjecture 2 as stated is not what Theorem 19 or consistency would use, or item (6) is false in the setting that matters.

**Fix.** State one stability/naturality conjecture in terms of the agreement relation ≈α of Definition 9, and make Conjecture 2 a corollary or special case. Say explicitly whether the containment in (6) is expected to hold.

### M4. The organising principle has exceptions, and they are defended by the kind of argument the paper itself discounts

*Where:* p. 15 §6.3; p. 16 (fourth `False`); p. 39 (η at `Unit`); pp. 52–53 note 25; p. 20 (ledger).

"No decision that changes a result is read from a normal form" is presented as the source of "most of Ochr's side conditions". Yet:

- Values at `Unit` are replaced by `()` when "the result of a call whose declared codomain, *or computed result type*, is `Unit`" (p. 39). That is a decision read from a computed type. It was half of the fourth closed proof of `False` (p. 16). The fix was to make generalisation records arm-local, not to change the η rule.
- Note 25 says a match on a proof is decided by the *refined stored type*, which "consults a normal form, but only in a way that fails safe".
- Two side conditions, non-cumulative universes and arm-local generalisation records, have no ledger switch (p. 20). The latter is the fix for the most recent `False`, so the regression evidence does not cover the newest soundness-critical rule.

"Fails safe" arguments about classes of programs are exactly what §7.2 says "is not a proof".

**Fix.**
- Give a precise, checkable formulation of the principle as a property of the rules.
- Enumerate every decision point, including the exceptions, in Conjecture 2's list.
- Add ledger switches for the two un-switchable conditions (or explain why they cannot exist).

### M5. The Aeneas comparison does not support the headline figures as stated

*Where:* Abstract; p. 2 ("of comparable size to the three pieces of the usual approach"); pp. 20–22 §8.2; Fig. 11 (p. 21).

1. **Outdated baseline.** The baseline is Aeneas's 2022 F* development. "Aeneas has since gained a Lean backend, which we have not compared against" (p. 20). The Lean backend is the natural comparison for a Lean-style type theory, and its hash map development is a ready baseline. Without it, the reader cannot tell whether the 29% "model + agreement" layer is intrinsic to the two-language approach, or an artefact of how the 2022 F* proofs happened to be structured.
2. **Author-defined categories.** The split into "Spec, model / Agreement / Property proofs" for Aeneas's file is a manual categorisation by the authors (the artefact contains the category map). It is not an objective measure. Several lemmas classed as agreement relate generated functions to *proof-internal* pure functions that an Aeneas user could have avoided.
3. **Not the same program, and not the same theorems.**
   - Ochr's numbers are unbounded, so Aeneas's overflow and failure reasoning (about 9% of its proof lines, p. 22) has no counterpart.
   - The load factor is fixed at one.
   - `Get`/`MapGetMut` take a proof that the key is present instead of returning an option.
   - Words are "unary in the logic, a machine word in the cost model" (p. 7). If compiled as machine words, this is an unsound abstraction unless overflow is excluded.
   - The Ochr hash map is a hand transliteration into a language whose semantics diverge from Rust's: pattern variables are places that see later writes (p. 8), and closures snapshot their captures (p. 10).
4. **Generalisation from one data point.** The intro's general claim ("even without automation, is of comparable size", p. 2) rests on this single development. Quicksort is 8x Verus's (Fig. 11). The paper explains this as the cost of no automation, but that is precisely the regime the intro claim covers.

**Fix.**
- Compare against Aeneas's Lean hash map.
- Either model bounded words or report what Aeneas's numbers become with overflow and failure reasoning removed.
- Present the line-count result as one data point with stated caveats, not a general claim.

### M6. The "in-place program" is never compiled, and its relationship to Rust is uncharacterised

*Where:* title; abstract; p. 2; p. 22 §8.3 (natives); p. 25 §10.2; Fig. 1 (p. 3).

The motivation is verifying the efficient program itself (§1.1). But:

- the only execution is the checker's reference interpreter;
- "That the compiled program behaves as the checked one is, for now, an assumption" (p. 25);
- array natives are trusted (p. 22);
- there is no compiler.

The paper also does not say how Ochr's acceptance relates to rustc's on the Fig. 1 fragment. Borrow checking is by running, path- and value-sensitive, and acceptance can depend on whether inputs are concrete (p. 3 §1.5). Some divergences are listed (returned-borrow regions, pattern-variable semantics, closures), but there is no statement of inclusion either way.

**Fix.**
- Temper the framing (for example, "the in-place program as executed by Ochr's machine").
- Ideally, state an erasure-correctness property for at least F: erased runs agree with runs that skip types and proofs (Lemma 18 is a start).
- Characterise, even informally, acceptance relative to Rust on the covered fragment.

### M7. Non-modular checking and non-compositional definitional equality deserve more than a scope paragraph

*Where:* p. 3 §1.5; p. 14 §6.2; p. 25 §10.1; p. 42 [T-Call].

- Callers are checked by running callee bodies.
- Conversion has no δ-rule for stuck calls, so "extracting a helper function can change which equations hold by refl" (p. 14).
- Opacity exists only for abstract function parameters.

For a system pitched at verifying real code, this affects proof robustness under refactoring and scalability, which are central to the thesis. The case studies are the place to measure it, yet §8 reports nothing about how often proofs broke under refactoring of callees, or how sealed-program sizes grow with call depth. Checking times are reported (about 3 s), but not the sizes of the normal forms.

**Fix.** Discuss and, ideally, measure these issues on the case studies, or at least sketch how an opaque/sealed-definition mechanism would interact with the two-path principle.

## 5. Minor concerns

1. **[T-Sort] contradicts the text and the checker.** [T-Sort] (p. 41) states `Prop : Type₀`. The body (p. 8, p. 10: "`Box(Prop)` is a universe error, since `Prop : Type₁`") and the sorts paragraph (p. 40: "`Type₁` for `Prop`") say `Prop : Type₁`. The checker uses `Prop : Type₁` by default (`Config.propUp := true`, `Env.lean:172`). This matters because the borrow restriction to `Type₀` relies on it.
2. **[End] is missing its wholeness premise.** [End] (Fig. 5 p. 9; A.2.1 p. 33) has no premise that the content is whole. The text says "[End] requires the content w to be whole" (p. 34) and "a borrow must be whole again... when it ends" (p. 10), while also saying of [End] "There is no side condition" (p. 10). Put the premise in the rule.
3. **Contradictory statements about reads in statements.** §10.2 (p. 25) says "Erased reads do not count as uses, which is what lets `x + x = 2 · x` be stated without clone". This contradicts p. 10 ("`Add(x, x)` must be written `Add(clone(x), x)`, in a type as in code") and p. 63 ("`Id(Nat, let z = a; a, a)` is rejected... since `a` is used twice"). The sentence appears to predate the current rule.
4. **The body omits dependent fields.** The body's description of inductive fields (p. 8) says they "may mention the type being declared, other declared types and the parameters". It never mentions dependent fields, yet the case studies depend on them (`Vec`, the hash map's sized array; p. 22). These fields bring in substantial machinery: open/repack, restricted injectivity, and proof fields set to ⊥ (A.4.8). This machinery is absent from the body and from Conjecture 2's list.
5. **Section 8.3 uses checker features outside the formal definition.** It relies on `abstract`, `unsized` and `implemented by` declarations and on runtime natives, none of which appear in Appendix A. The case studies therefore rest on checker features outside the formal calculus. Also, `Word` is "declared copy", and its logical/compiled meaning is not formalised.
6. **Overstated claim about convertibility.** p. 14 says two functions with the same body and different names are not convertible. By [Conv-fun] (p. 39) they are convertible when their bodies do not get stuck at the generic call; the claim holds only for stuck bodies.
7. **Fuzzer numbers are stale.** The fuzzer statistics refer to "17 of the then 20 soundness rows" (p. 19), whereas Fig. 10 has 23. Please re-run against the current ledger. Also state whether the generator covers dependent fields (A.4.8), `rewrite`, `split f in t`, and abstract/unsized types. From a look at the artefact's generator, it seems not to generate `rewrite`, goal `split` or dependent fields.
8. **The termination comparison with Lean understates Ochr's risk.** On p. 18 the termination discussion says "Lean is in the same position". This understates the matter: Ochr has additional divergence risks from re-normalising and re-closing sealed programs, one of which was found and fixed (note 8, p. 51).
9. **Non-standard truth definition.** Definition 7 (p. 55) counts stuck equations as true. Corollary 21 then "says nothing" about places a side moves out (p. 62). Theorem 4 in the body (p. 19) should state this restriction. Should an `Id` whose side moves an observed place out simply be rejected?
10. **Unclear status of the injectivity claim.** p. 25 says "Ochr proves that every `Π(x : &Nat). &Nat` has an injective one" (backward function). Please clarify whether this is an internal theorem or a model property. Either way, the consequence that any opaque/FFI function of that type that leaks a borrow is an inconsistent axiom deserves emphasis in §10.
11. **Is ≡ an equivalence relation?** Conversion is "the least relation closed under" five rules (p. 39), with no symmetry or transitivity rules. Is ≡ provably an equivalence relation, in particular with [Conv-unit] and [Conv-fun] interleaved? The checker presumably relies on this.
12. **Duplicate numbering and forward references.** The same result is Theorem 4 in the body (p. 19) and Theorem 19 in the appendix (p. 60). Assumptions 10/11 are cited in the body before they are stated. Fig. 9 (p. 17) cites [Seal] and `FootprintProbe`, which the body never introduces.
13. **Value/place confusion.** On p. 6, `Le(q, p)` names the place `p` where the value (the abstract predecessor) is meant.
14. **Overstated slogan.** The title/abstract claim "own specification" is a slight overstatement: statements still need specification vocabulary (`Sorted`, `Count`, `OnlyIn`, `Find`), 184 lines for the hash map (Fig. 11). The precise claim, "no model and no agreement proof", is fine; the slogan should not exceed it.
15. **Imprecise statement of the fire triangle.** p. 2 summarises it as showing such a theory "is inconsistent". The result is that the three cannot coexist without giving one up; please phrase it precisely.

## 6. Presentation and readability

**Overview.** The overview (§2) is the best part of the paper: concrete, well paced, and motivating.

**Prose density.** From §4 onward the prose is extremely dense. Long sentences chain many clauses and bracketed rule names, often forward-referencing appendix sections. Many key definitions appear in the body only as prose summaries:

- footprints;
- owners;
- erasure classes;
- stuck-block capture modes;
- generalisation records;
- the declared-type discipline.

The actual definitions are in a 37-page appendix (pp. 29–65). A reader who does not read the appendix cannot check any rule. A reader who does faces rules interleaved with checker function names and design history.

**Process narrative.** Sections 6.3–6.7 and 7.2 read partly as a changelog:

- "an earlier version", "a later review", "the fourth that a cold reader has found";
- "65 findings of one class... were re-triaged";
- internal decision identifiers leaking into the text: (D53), (D58), (D68) on pp. 45, 53, 54, 58, 62, 63;
- test and probe names such as `AssignBot`, `RetLocal`, `Trav`, `FootprintProbe`.

The history is valuable, but it belongs in a clearly separated section or the artefact, not inside the definitions.

**Formalism.** There is no declarative system: "the machine run with types, is the definition" (p. 14). This is defensible, but it makes the metatheory hard to state and to compare with existing type theories. A small declarative presentation of F, even if incomplete, would help readers.

**Figures.**
- Figs. 9, 10 and 16 are useful.
- Fig. 1 floats into §2's introduction and leaves a large blank region on p. 3.
- Several appendix rules overflow or collide with their labels (see §8).

**Page budget.** The body fits about 25 pages, but leans heavily on the appendix for anything checkable.

## 7. Questions for the authors

1. Can Assumption 10 be proved for F as it stands, and what is the status of that effort? Which of the three listed risks (p. 56) do you consider most likely to fail?
2. How should Conjecture 2 be restated in terms of the agreement relation ≈α (Definition 9), given that footprints may differ in both directions (p. 63)?
3. How many soundness-relevant bugs have been found in total, by date? Has the rate fallen? Has any fuzzing campaign targeted dependent fields, `J`/`rewrite`/`split`, and the abstract/native mechanism?
4. Why is η at `Unit` decided by a *computed* result type (p. 39), given the principle of §6.3? Would a declared-only rule lose any case-study proof?
5. What does the comparison with Aeneas's Lean-backend hash map development look like?
6. If `Word` is unbounded in the logic, what is the intended compilation story, and how would overflow be handled?
7. On the Fig. 1 fragment, is every Ochr-accepted program rustc-accepted, or vice versa? Can you give an example of each direction of divergence beyond those already listed?
8. Is "Ochr proves... an injective one" (p. 25) an internal theorem? Can you exhibit the Ochr proof term?
9. How large do sealed programs get in the case studies (nesting depth, term size)? Did any proofs break when callees were refactored during the case studies?
10. Is ≡ transitive as defined (least relation, no explicit transitivity)?
11. In the hash map, how were Aeneas's lines assigned to "agreement" versus "property proofs", and how sensitive is the 36% figure to that assignment?
