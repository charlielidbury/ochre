This is the document's self-declared heart, and I believe the written proof doesn't go through as stated. In the MC tier case it fixes j′ < m and c ∈ ⟨a₃⟩ⱼ′, then claims the second hypothesis gives [c]b₂ ∈ ⟦[c]b₃⟧ⱼ″ for all j″ because "its tiers are available at every index". But the tier of MATCH_{m′}(v_y, v_z) at index j″ only quantifies over c ∈ ⟨a₃⟩_{j″} — and goodness is antitone (Lemma 4.1: higher index, smaller set), so a c that is good at depth j′ need not be good at any j″ > j′. Finitely-good "junk" arguments exist (e.g. a term that weak-head-reaches a stuck spine after j′ steps). So the inner MT application, which genuinely needs its second hypothesis at indices above the middle body's evaluation cost i — and i can exceed j′ — has nothing to instantiate. The middle-term-budget problem the construction dissolves at top level reappears one binder down, because inside MATCH the ∀-fact is capped by the argument's goodness depth.

This is the classic transitivity problem of step-indexed logical relations, and the proposed budget-left/quantify-right asymmetry doesn't inherit through the tier clause. Notably, the FT itself never trips over this: every argument it feeds into a tier is derivation-backed and therefore good at all depths (that's exactly how W-APP and DS-APP go through). The obvious repair — restricting MATCH's tiers to ∀-good arguments — breaks the well-foundedness of the definition (it reintroduces the unbounded negative occurrence that the grading exists to prevent). So the options are roughly: find a cleverer formulation of MT/MC (e.g. a hereditarily-∀ invariant threaded through the FT, exploiting that DS-TRANS's middle term carries its own instrumented derivation), or restructure the model, or find a countermodel to MT as stated. I tried a quick countermodel and didn't land one — the (m2) "type side must converge to a value" clause kills the easy attempts — so the theorem may well be true with a repaired proof; it's the proof that's gapped.
